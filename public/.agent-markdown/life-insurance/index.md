## Plan your life insurance quote

### Start with what your family would need

List the income, debts and expenses your family would need help with. Note which needs have an end date, such as the years left on a mortgage, and which could last longer, such as support for a dependent. Account for savings and existing insurance before choosing an amount and reviewing beneficiaries.

### Compare the same benefit and costs

Start with the same death benefit on each quote. For term life, compare the initial term and what happens when it ends. For permanent life, ask how long you would pay, what is guaranteed, and what could happen if you pay less or miss a payment. Use the insurer’s secure application for health details.

Can I convert term life insurance to a permanent policy?

Some term policies include a conversion option. Check the deadline, eligible permanent products and new premium with the insurer. A conversion right is not included in every policy; review the written conditions before your term ends.

How much life insurance do I need?

Start with the income your family would need to replace, debts, housing costs, childcare and future expenses. Subtract savings and existing coverage that would be available to them. Review both the amount and how long the need may last; one income multiple does not fit every family.

How much does life insurance cost in Miami?

Life insurance pricing depends on age, health, tobacco use, coverage amount, policy type and term length. Compare quotes for the same death benefit and duration, and ask whether premiums can change. Choose a payment you can sustain; a preliminary quote is not the insurer’s final offer or a guarantee of approval.

Do I need a medical exam to apply for life insurance?

It depends on the insurer, product and your application. Some options may use health questions or records without an exam, but no-exam does not mean automatic approval. Call to discuss the application process; do not send medical records through this public website.

Is life insurance through my employer enough?

Check the benefit amount, who receives it and what happens if you change jobs or stop working. Employer coverage may help, but it may not meet every household expense or remain available after employment ends. Include it when reviewing your family’s overall needs.

What should I compare in whole life or universal life insurance?

Whole life and universal life are types of permanent insurance with different guarantees and payment terms. When a quote includes an illustration, compare the guaranteed and projected values in the same policy years. Ask how long you would pay, what you could receive if you cancel, and how missed payments or a loan could affect the policy.

[NAIC guide to life insurance illustrations](<https://content.naic.org/insurance-topics/life-insurance-illustrations>)

Can life insurance include living benefits?

Some policies offer an accelerated death benefit for a qualifying illness, subject to the policy or rider. Using it can reduce the death benefit left for beneficiaries. Ask about eligibility, limits and costs; a living benefit is not a general substitute for health insurance.
