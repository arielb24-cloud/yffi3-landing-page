COI Guide

# Certificate of Insurance Request Guidance

A certificate generally provides evidence of coverage. It does not replace the policy or create terms that the underlying contract does not provide.

Customer checklist

## Your Checklist

Use these steps to prepare your questions and documents. Your policy and insurer determine what is covered.

### Get the request in writing

Ask the requester for the exact certificate holder name, address, project or contract reference, required limits, and any requested endorsements.

### Send documents securely

Do not post policy numbers or contracts on this public site. Use an approved office, carrier, or secure document channel.

### Separate evidence from coverage

A certificate documents policy information as issued. It cannot add coverage, change limits, waive exclusions, or amend the policy by itself.

### Review contract language

Additional insured, waiver, primary/noncontributory, notice, vehicle, workers compensation, or project requirements may require carrier approval or an endorsement.

### Allow processing time

Submit complete written requirements before a deadline. Complex or unsupported requests may need underwriting review or policy changes.

### Verify the final document

Check the named insured, certificate holder, carrier, policy dates, limits, description, and any attached endorsements before delivery.

FAQ

## Certificate of insurance questions

What is a certificate of insurance, or COI?

A COI summarizes evidence of insurance, such as the named insured, insurer, policy dates and limits. Businesses often request one before a job or lease. The policy and endorsements determine the coverage; the certificate itself does not change them.

What information do I need to request a certificate?

Prepare the certificate holder’s exact name and address, the written insurance requirements, project or contract reference and deadline. Include any requested additional insured or other endorsement wording. Call for instructions on securely sharing the contract and policy details.

[Call 305-910-8850](tel:13059108850)

Is a certificate holder the same as an additional insured?

No. Being listed as a certificate holder does not automatically make someone an additional insured. Additional insured status must come from the policy or an applicable endorsement. Have the actual requirement reviewed before promising it to a client or landlord.

Can a certificate add coverage or raise my liability limit?

No. A certificate cannot increase limits, remove exclusions or certify coverage the policy does not provide. If the contract asks for something different, the policy may need an approved change before the certificate can reflect it.

How long does a certificate request take?

Timing depends on whether the request matches existing coverage or needs insurer approval or an endorsement. Send complete requirements before the job or lease deadline. Call to confirm the request’s status; do not assume coverage or a certificate is ready until confirmed.
